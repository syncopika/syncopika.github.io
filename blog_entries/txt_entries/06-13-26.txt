I went to London, England for the first time for about a week from 6/5 - 6/12! I stayed in the Earl's Court area, which is a great location for being directly connected to Heathrow airport while still being close enough to central London and all the attractions!

--p
I took British Airways for the first time from IAD <-> LHR. I flew on a 777-200 and the flight time was ~7.5 hours going there and ~8 hours on the way back.

--p
Overall British Airways was not bad - service was fine, food was fine, seats were fine. IFE was just ok though. It didn't seem like you could toggle subtitles for movies - not sure if intentional or if not, isn't that an accessibility issue? On the return flight, the 777 I flew on had an even older IFE system I think, with the old remote and non-touch screen monitor lol.

--p
On the way to London, I had butter chicken with okra as the main for the meal. On the way back I had pasta.

The weather in London was ok! I experienced mostly cloudiness with some rain sprinkled in, as well as with some sunshine too so it was not too bad overall in my opinion. It was pretty windy at times but not too cold so you could still wear shorts a lot of the time :). The temperature was pretty consistently around the 50s to low 60s (Fahrenheit). I definitely needed a jacket though and an umbrella.

--p
British food was fine - not terrible, not bad, but I guess nothing much to write home about lol. For sure the flavors weren't particularly exotic but they weren't as bad I think as some people make it out to be. I also tried some UK-specific fast food items like the Wagyu burger (which came in a nice box) and halloumi cheese fries from Burger King.

The most important thing though was the sights and museums! There's so much art and history to see and take in in London :D. All the museums are free, but some sights like the Tower of London and St. Paul's Cathedral charge an admission fee, which is a bit steep but worth it in my opinion. And of course everywhere throughout the city is old, historic architecture which is really cool. Below are some of the neat places I visited.

St. Paul's Cathedral
--p 
There was a nice view that required going up 528 steps (most were very small, steep and narrow steps in very tight spaces which made it not an easy hike!) but it was worth it, even though it was raining. The highest point is called the Golden Galley. There is also a crypt that featured the tombs of some notable people (and also conveniently contained the gift shop, cafe and exit).
